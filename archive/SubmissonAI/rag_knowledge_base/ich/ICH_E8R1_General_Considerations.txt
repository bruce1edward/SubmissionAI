ICH E8(R1) GUIDELINE: GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS FOR CLINICAL STUDIES
Effective Date: October 6, 2021
Regulatory Body: ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION

This revision of ICH E8 updates the framework for designing quality clinical studies that generate reliable, decision-relevant evidence. The central concept introduced in the revised guideline is quality by design (QbD) for clinical trials, which emphasizes identifying and managing factors critical to the quality of a trial before and during its conduct rather than discovering quality problems after the trial is complete.

SECTION 2: QUALITY IN CLINICAL TRIALS

2.1 Definition of Quality
Quality in the context of clinical trials means the absence of features that diminish the utility of the study: errors that materially affect the key decisions made on the basis of the trial results, or ethical violations. Quality is not the same as perfection or the absence of any deviation. Quality exists when a trial produces trustworthy evidence for the decisions it is designed to support.

2.2 Factors Critical to Quality
Critical factors are those features of the study that, if compromised, would have a substantial impact on the protection of trial participants or the reliability of the data used for decision-making. The identification of critical factors should occur during trial design and should drive risk management and monitoring strategies.

Not all protocol deviations are equally important. Deviations from critical factors require immediate attention and may invalidate portions of the data. Deviations from non-critical elements may be documented without affecting the overall trial validity.

2.3 Proportionate Approaches to Quality Management
The extent and nature of quality assurance activities should be proportionate to the risks associated with the study. Routine monitoring visits at every site for every trial may not be the most efficient approach to quality. Risk-based monitoring focuses oversight on the sites and data elements most likely to affect participant safety and data reliability.

SECTION 3: PLANNING A CLINICAL STUDY

3.1 Study Objectives and Clinical Questions
The study objective specifies what the study is designed to demonstrate. A well-formed clinical question includes: the patient population, the intervention, the comparator, the outcome measure, and the timing of measurement. The ICH E9(R1) estimand framework provides a structured approach to translating clinical questions into statistical estimands.

3.2 Study Design Elements
Each design element must be chosen to answer the study objective with adequate reliability:

(a) Type of study: Interventional studies may be controlled or uncontrolled. Controlled studies may use a placebo, active, or dose-response control. The design type must match the research question.

(b) Endpoints: The primary endpoint must be clinically meaningful, validly measured, and sensitive to the treatment effect. Surrogate endpoints require evidence of their relationship to the clinical outcome of interest.

(c) Duration: Study duration must be sufficient to capture the clinically relevant outcome. For chronic disease trials, duration sufficient to observe durability of response is important.

(d) Population: The enrolled population must be relevant to the intended use of the drug and must be representative of the population in which the drug will be used clinically.

3.3 Scientific Basis for Design Choices
The rationale for each design choice must be documented in the protocol. This includes the basis for the choice of comparator, the endpoint measurement method, the sample size assumptions, and the statistical analysis approach. External data sources used in design decisions (e.g., historical control data, literature estimates) must be cited.

SECTION 4: CONDUCT OF CLINICAL STUDIES

4.1 Protocol Adherence
Protocol adherence is fundamental to generating reliable evidence. Sites must be selected based on their ability to comply with protocol requirements and enroll the target population. Pre-study site assessments and training are essential.

4.2 Data Integrity
Data must be collected using validated, fit-for-purpose instruments. Electronic data capture systems must be validated. Source data verification should focus on data critical to the primary endpoint and subject safety. Data queries must be resolved in a timely manner.

4.3 Participant Protection
The protection of clinical trial participants is the highest priority. All studies must be conducted in accordance with GCP, the Declaration of Helsinki, and applicable regulations. Independent ethics committee approval must be obtained before enrollment begins. Informed consent must be an ongoing process throughout the study.

SECTION 5: ANALYSIS AND REPORTING

5.1 Pre-Specified Analysis
The statistical analysis plan must be pre-specified and finalized before database lock. Any analysis conducted after database lock that was not pre-specified must be clearly identified as post-hoc in the study report.

5.2 Transparency and Completeness
All pre-specified analyses must be reported, including those with unfavorable results. Selective reporting of statistically significant results while omitting pre-specified analyses that failed to reach significance is a form of research misconduct that creates a misleading evidence base.

5.3 Clinical Study Reports
The clinical study report must be organized in accordance with ICH E3 and must include: a complete accounting of all subjects, all pre-specified analyses with results, all safety data, and a balanced discussion of findings including limitations.

SECTION 6: ROLE OF SPONSORS, INVESTIGATORS, AND REGULATORY AUTHORITIES

6.1 Sponsor Responsibilities
The sponsor is responsible for implementing and maintaining quality standards throughout the clinical program. The sponsor must ensure that the trial is designed to produce reliable evidence, that the protocol and SAP are scientifically sound, that sites are qualified and properly trained, and that the data management and analysis processes are rigorous.

6.2 Investigator Responsibilities
The investigator is responsible for the conduct of the study at the site, the protection of subjects, and the integrity of the data collected at the site. Investigators must be qualified by education, training, and experience to conduct the research. They must conduct the study in accordance with the protocol, GCP, and applicable regulations.

6.3 Regulatory Authority Engagement
Early engagement with regulatory authorities (e.g., through Type B meetings with FDA or scientific advice with EMA) can prevent costly design errors in pivotal trials. Sponsors are encouraged to seek regulatory input on trial design, endpoints, and statistical analysis plans for Phase 3 studies that will form the primary basis for marketing approval.

SECTION 7: INNOVATIVE TRIAL DESIGNS

Adaptive designs, master protocols, platform trials, and basket trials offer opportunities to generate efficient evidence, but require careful planning and regulatory consultation. The adaptations must be pre-specified and must not introduce operational or statistical bias. Simulation studies demonstrating Type I error control are generally expected for adaptive designs seeking regulatory support.
